## Purpose

The Executive Design Language (EDL) defines how information is visually communicated across Leadership Nexus reports and coaching deliverables.

It exists to support better coaching conversations. Every visual element should earn its place on the page.

---

## Guiding Principle

Before introducing any component, ask:

> "Does this help a coach prepare for a better coaching conversation?"

If the answer is no, simplify it or remove it.

The report should help a coach understand the participant's current leadership state in less than five minutes.

Information should flow naturally from:

1. Context
2. Insight
3. Evidence
4. Coaching Priorities
5. Development Actions

The report should never feel like a business intelligence dashboard.

It should feel like an executive leadership briefing.

---

## Design Philosophy

The design should embody the Leadership Nexus philosophy:

- **Alignment creates effectiveness.**
- **Clarity creates confidence.**
- **Development happens through intentional action.**

Whenever there is a choice between adding information and improving clarity, always choose clarity.

---

## Visual Standards

### Typography

- Small muted text for labels.
- Stronger text for values and headlines.
- Narrative text should be readable at standard print scale.
- Headlines should be concise and declarative.

### Color

- Leadership Nexus navy: `#1e3a5f` — used for titles and section headers.
- Leadership Nexus green: `#2d7a4f` — used for positive emphasis, accent values, and alignment indicators.
- Neutral grays for labels, dividers, and secondary text.
- Avoid large colored backgrounds, shadows, or decorative cards.

### Layout

- US Letter (8.5" × 11") print-ready canvas.
- 12-column grid for flexible section placement.
- Clean dividers over cards or containers.
- Executive briefing aesthetic — no dashboard widgets.

### Spacing

- Generous whitespace between major sections.
- Compact metadata rows with thin dividers.
- Section titles in small caps with letter-spacing.
- Consistent vertical rhythm throughout.

---

## Success Measure

The ultimate measure of success is not visual appeal — it is whether the report helps a coach facilitate a more meaningful leadership conversation.
